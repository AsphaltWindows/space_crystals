# Play-testing M5: the Cults

Slice M5-M writes these notes in full. Until then, each slice adds what to try with what it built.

## The Airstrip and the Sparrow (slice M5-N7)

- **Getting one.** Recruits build the Airstrip from their Advanced Construct menu: E, then D (250 crystals, 40 s),
  once their player owns a Chop Shop. The Chop Shop comes with slice M5-N6, so until then D stays greyed ("Requires a
  Chop Shop") and a normal game has no Airstrip. To try one before then, make a **Custom** map in the map editor
  (`cargo run -- --editor`, Custom in the new map's dialog: only a Custom map may place objects for a slot), place an
  Airstrip and a few Recruits for slot 1, with crystal patches and a Storage to pay for the Sparrows (the Cults start
  with no Space Crystals), save it and start it with `--map maps/<name>.json --factions cults,<faction>`.
- **Its look.** A 4 × 2 runway (a dark strip down its length, a placeholder). Selected, it shows its door arrows: in
  at side B, where Recruits walk in, and out at side C, the end its Sparrows take off toward.
- **Training.** Right-click Recruits onto it (the Enter pointer): they walk in at B. Its panel is the Armory's: pick a
  kind of Recruit first (Q Plain, W Constitution, E Metabolism, R Both, each with its count inside), then Q builds a
  Sparrow (75 crystals, 20 s) of the Recruit of that kind inside the longest, and A releases one unchanged. In the
  main panel A ejects every free Recruit (they walk out of side C), X cancels the last order with a full refund, and
  C sets the rally point, also while it's still being built. It holds 10 Recruits, the reserved ones counting, so 10
  orders at most.
- **Takeoff.** A finished Sparrow appears over the runway's B end, facing C, and speeds up along the runway. Past the
  C end it turns for the rally point, or with none it circles 2 grid units past C. It never waits for the door: a
  Recruit standing in it doesn't hold it back. It comes out at full HP, whatever the Recruit's wounds, and the
  Recruit's augmentations are lost. It never lands: a right-click on the Airstrip only sends it there to circle.
- **The Sparrow.** A small glider: it can't stop, and circles when idle. Stop circles where it is; Hold circles and
  fires only at what comes in range. Its machine gun hits ground and air (3 damage, 3 grid units). On an attack it
  makes strafing passes: through the target, on until the target's centre is out of range, a loop back at full speed
  (about 490 su out, within its 8 grid units of sight), and again, until the target dies.
- **What shoots it down.** Militants, Guards and the Laser Turret, whose attacks hit air. Peacekeepers, Agents and the
  Colonists' Sabre and Rifle Legionnaires can't: their attacks are ground only. The GDO's Rocketman, its answer to it
  (design change 0356), comes with slice M5-K.
- **The Colonists' gap** (design change 0356): the Colonists have nothing that hits air, and won't until the Cults
  roster is done. A Colonists player can't fight Sparrows back; that is known and accepted for this playtest.

Try: a wave of Sparrows against Militants (they outrange the Sparrows, 4 grid units to 3) and against Guards; a
Sparrow sent to attack a building or a lone unit with nothing else of yours in sight (it should keep making passes
until the target dies); the Airstrip destroyed with Sparrows in the air (they fly on) and Recruits inside (they come
out, the orders refunded).

## The Minimap (slice M5-MM, design change 0415)

- **What it shows.** The whole map in the HUD's bottom-left panel, its terrain flat, each tile in its own colour:
  buildings as their footprints and units as dots in their player's colour, crystal patches and the Supply Delivery
  Station in a neutral grey. The fog is the map's: black where the side you are on has never looked; dimmed where it
  has looked before, showing only the buildings and patches it remembers, as it last saw them; units and everything
  live only where it sees now. Hidden things (a Syndicate Hideout, a unit underground) don't show for those who can't
  see them. F3 shows everything; F2 shows the other side's.
- **Using it.** A left-click centres the camera on that point, a drag pans as you go; the white outline is the
  camera's view. A right-click is the selection's right-click on that ground, and after A a left-click is the Attack
  Move there. It never selects anything, and never places a building.
- **Not yet.** Attack alerts on the minimap (not decided in the design).

Try: scout an enemy base and come back, and see it stay on the minimap, dimmed, as you last saw it; select units,
right-click the minimap far away, and watch them go; drag across the minimap to pan; with A, then a click on the
minimap, send an army across the map.
