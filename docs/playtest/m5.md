# Play-testing M5: the Cults

Slice M5-M writes these notes in full. Until then, each slice adds what to try with what it built.

## The Airstrip and the Sparrow (slice M5-N7)

- **Getting one.** Recruits build the Airstrip from their Advanced Construct menu: E, then D (250 crystals, 40 s),
  once their player owns a Chop Shop. Since slice M5-N6 the Recruits build the Chop Shop too (below), so a normal
  game has an Airstrip now: an Armory, then a Chop Shop, then the Airstrip. To skip the build, make a **Custom** map
  in the map editor (`cargo run -- --editor`, Custom in the new map's dialog: only a Custom map may place objects for
  a slot), place an Airstrip and a few Recruits for slot 1, with crystal patches and a Storage to pay for the
  Sparrows (the Cults start with no Space Crystals), save it and start it with
  `--map maps/<name>.json --factions cults,<faction>`.
- **Its look.** A 4 × 2 runway (a dark strip down its length, a placeholder). Selected, it shows its door arrows: in
  at side B, where Recruits walk in, and out at side C, the end its Sparrows take off toward.
- **Training.** Right-click Recruits onto it (the Enter pointer): they walk in at B. Its panel is the Armory's: pick a
  kind of Recruit first (Q Plain, W Constitution, E Metabolism, R Both, each with its count inside), then Q builds a
  Sparrow (75 crystals, 20 s) of the Recruit of that kind inside the longest, and A releases one unchanged. In the
  main panel A ejects every free Recruit (they walk out of side C), X cancels the last order with a full refund, and
  C sets the rally point, also while it's still being built. It holds 10 Recruits, the reserved ones counting, so 10
  orders at most.
- **Takeoff.** A finished Sparrow appears over the runway's B end, facing C, and speeds up along the runway. Past the
  C end it turns for the rally point, or with none it circles 2 grid units past C. It never waits for the door: a
  Recruit standing in it doesn't hold it back. It comes out at full HP, whatever the Recruit's wounds, and the
  Recruit's augmentations are lost. It never lands: a right-click on the Airstrip only sends it there to circle.
- **The Sparrow.** A small glider: it can't stop, and circles when idle. Stop circles where it is; Hold circles and
  fires only at what comes in range. Its machine gun hits ground and air (3 damage, 3 grid units). On an attack it
  makes strafing passes: through the target, on until the target's centre is out of range, a loop back at full speed
  (about 490 su out, within its 8 grid units of sight), and again, until the target dies.
- **What shoots it down.** Militants, Guards and the Laser Turret, whose attacks hit air, and the GDO's Rocketman
  (slice M5-K, below). Peacekeepers, Agents and the Colonists' Sabre and Rifle Legionnaires can't: their attacks are
  ground only.
- **The Colonists' gap** (design change 0356): the Colonists have nothing that hits air, and won't until the Cults
  roster is done. A Colonists player can't fight Sparrows back; that is known and accepted for this playtest.

Try: a wave of Sparrows against Militants (they outrange the Sparrows, 4 grid units to 3) and against Guards; a
Sparrow sent to attack a building or a lone unit with nothing else of yours in sight (it should keep making passes
until the target dies); the Airstrip destroyed with Sparrows in the air (they fly on) and Recruits inside (they come
out, the orders refunded).

## The Arsenal and the Rocketman (slices M5-K and M5-S8, design changes 0356 and 0445)

The GDO's anti-air, with placeholder numbers.

- **Getting one.** The Deployment Center's Basic menu has the Arsenal in S (150 crystals, 20 s), greyed "Requires a
  Barracks" until one stands. It is 2 × 2, draws 20 Power and has no panel. Once it stands, the Barracks' W trains
  the Rocketman (100 crystals, 10 s); until then W is greyed, "Requires an Arsenal". Losing the last Arsenal greys W
  again, and the Rocketmen already queued still come out.
- **The missile.** The Rocketman (45 HP, a little slower than a Peacekeeper) fires a missile at ground or air, every
  2 seconds, from 2 to 6 grid units. Closer than 2, it backs off before it fires. The missile flies to where its
  target was when the Rocketman finished aiming, so a target that moves dodges it. A Sparrow never stops, so it
  dodges nearly every one.
- **The burst.** Every missile, normal or locked, bursts where it lands: 40 damage shared over a circle of 32 su (half
  a grid unit), each object taking the part of it that its shape covers: about 7 to an infantry unit inside it, 20 to
  a Sparrow, 30 to a vehicle hit square-on, before armour. It hits whoever stands in it, your own units included. A
  missile fired at an aircraft bursts in the air and hits only aircraft; one fired at a ground unit or at the ground
  hits only what is on (or under) the ground. So a missile at a Sparrow that flew on bursts harmlessly, even over
  your units; a locked missile on a Sparrow in a tight group hurts the Sparrows next to it a little.
- **R: Target Lock.** R, then a left-click on an enemy unit or building you see now, locks every selected Rocketman
  onto it. A locking Rocketman stands still and fires nothing for 3 seconds (an amber arc fills round the target, to
  you only). Once locked (a red ring), every missile it fires at that target homes in and can't miss, from anywhere
  within 7 grid units, even closer than 2. The lock breaks when the target goes beyond 7 grid units, when you lose
  sight of it, when the target dies, or when you give the Rocketman any order without Shift. With Shift, an order waits
  until the lock ends: Shift+R onto a second target queues a lock on it for when the first dies.
- **Keep it in sight.** A Rocketman sees 6 grid units, less than its lock's 7, so it loses sight of a target before
  it leaves range. A Sparrow strafing your Rocketmen loops out of their sight on every pass, and their lock breaks
  before it completes. With your buildings or other units seeing the Sparrow's loops, the locks hold, and three
  Rocketmen bring a Sparrow down with three locked missiles. A Sparrow circling on Hold within their sight falls the
  same way.
- **Its look.** Placeholders: a missile is a small body in its owner's colour with a flame. A locked one curves after
  its target. Where a missile lands, its burst shows as a grenade's blast does, smaller, its ring in the Rocketman's
  owner's colour; a burst at an aircraft shows in the air. If the target dies while a locked missile is in flight,
  the missile flies on to the target's last spot and bursts there, hitting only what is there of the target's kind
  (an aircraft, or what is on the ground).

Try: Rocketmen against a wave of Sparrows, without and then with buildings or other units watching the Sparrows'
loops; Shift+R onto each Sparrow in turn; Rocketmen into a clump of enemy infantry with some of yours among them; a lock
on a building (it can target buildings); a lock broken by sending the target out of range, by moving the Rocketman, and
by losing sight of the target; W at the Barracks before and after the Arsenal stands, and after it is destroyed.

## The Minimap (slices M5-MM and M5-MM2, design changes 0415 and 0443)

- **What it shows.** The whole map in the HUD's bottom-left panel, its terrain flat, each tile in its own colour:
  buildings as their footprints and units as dots in their player's colour, crystal patches and the Supply Delivery
  Station in a neutral grey. The fog is the map's: black where the side you are on has never looked; dimmed where it
  has looked before, showing only the buildings and patches it remembers, as it last saw them; units and everything
  live only where it sees now. Hidden things (a Syndicate Hideout, a unit underground) don't show for those who can't
  see them. F3 shows everything; F2 shows the other side's.
- **Using it.** A left-click centres the camera on that point, a drag pans as you go; the white outline is the
  camera's view. A right-click is the selection's right-click on that ground, and after A a left-click is the Attack
  Move there. It never selects anything, and never places a building.
- **Alerts** (slice M5-MM2, design change 0443). When a unit or building of yours takes a hit outside the camera's
  view (outside the white outline), a red ring pings on the minimap at its spot: it shrinks and fades for 2 seconds,
  with no text and no sound. Any hit counts, Shields only too. Placeholders: a fight in one place pings at most once
  every 10 seconds (a hit within 8 grid units of a ping of the last 10 seconds makes none, though that ping has
  faded); a fight elsewhere pings at once. **Space** centres the camera on the latest ping, faded or not; before the
  first, it does nothing. The pings are the side's you are on: after F2, the other side's hits ping, and Space goes
  to that side's latest. In a replay, K pauses now (it was Space).

Try: scout an enemy base and come back, and see it stay on the minimap, dimmed, as you last saw it; select units,
right-click the minimap far away, and watch them go; drag across the minimap to pan; with A, then a click on the
minimap, send an army across the map. Let the computer attack an outpost while you look at your base, watch for the
ping, and press Space; keep a long fight going and count its pings; fight in two places at once.

## Full screen, the playtest menu and the explored map (slice M5-S7, design changes 0413, 0414 and 0416)

- **Full screen.** The game opens in borderless full screen; F11 or Alt+Enter switches to a window and back
  (placeholder keys), and `--windowed` starts in a window. The cursor stays in the game while it is the active
  window, in full screen and in a window, so moving it to the edge pans the camera (within 8 pixels); Alt+Tab frees
  it.
- **The playtest menu** (temporary). F10 pauses the game under a small menu: Resume (or F10), Restart, Quit. At the
  end of a game it opens by itself under the result, with Restart and Quit. Restart starts the same game again from
  0:00, computer players included, and records it to a replay of its own.
- **The map explored.** Every game starts with the whole map explored: the terrain and the crystal patches show
  dimmed where you don't see, as they are at the start, and the other bases don't show until you scout them.
  `--unexplored` turns it off.

Try: start in full screen on a second monitor and pan at the edge between the screens; switch to a window and back
with F11 and with Alt+Enter; F10 mid-fight and check that nothing moves until Resume; restart a game against the
computer and play it again; look across the explored map at the start and scout an enemy base you couldn't see.

## The Chop Shop and the Seeker (slices M5-N6 and M5-S8, design changes 0446 and 0447)

- **Getting one.** Recruits build the Chop Shop from their Advanced Construct menu: E, then A (200 crystals, 40 s),
  once their player owns an Armory (Basic Construct: W, then A). In a normal game: an Armory, then a Chop Shop, then
  Seekers. The Chop Shop unlocks the Airstrip (above) and the Machine Shop, whose entries are no longer greyed once
  it stands.
- **Its look.** A 3 × 3 structure labelled "CS" (a placeholder). Selected, it shows its door arrows: in at side B,
  where Recruits walk in, and out at side C, where its Seekers drive out.
- **Its rally point.** A new Chop Shop already has a rally point, 3 grid units straight out from the middle of side
  C, wherever you turned or flipped it (the distance is a placeholder). It is there from the moment you place it,
  while it is still being built: select the site and you see the green marker and its line. It is an ordinary rally
  point: right-click the ground to move it, or right-click the Chop Shop itself (or C, then a click on it) to clear
  it. Placed against the map's edge, it sits on the edge instead (a placeholder); on water or a cliff it stays where
  it is.
- **Training.** Right-click Recruits onto it (the Enter pointer): they walk in at B. Its panel is the Armory's: pick a
  kind of Recruit first (Q Plain, W Constitution, E Metabolism, R Both, each with its count inside), then Q builds a
  Seeker (75 crystals, 20 s) of **two** Recruits of that kind, the two inside the longest; Q is greyed while fewer
  than two of the kind are free ("No free unit of that kind"). A releases one Recruit unchanged. In the main panel A
  ejects every free Recruit (they walk out of side C), X cancels the last order with a full refund (its two Recruits
  go back to the pool), and C sets the rally point, also while it's still being built. It holds 10 Recruits, the
  reserved ones counting, so 5 Seekers queued at most.
- **Out.** A finished Seeker appears half inside the middle of side C, facing out, and drives straight out to the
  rally point. Seekers you leave alone park one behind the other between the rally point and the door, so the door
  stays free. With the rally point cleared it stops just outside the door. It comes out at full HP, whatever its
  Recruits' wounds, and their augmentations are lost. Its Unit Control is 2, a point at each of its two Recruits'
  Recruitment Halls: if one of those Halls goes, half of it is an orphan.
- **The Seeker.** A wheeled buggy, 56 × 40, 120 HP. It speeds up to 8 su a tick in 8 ticks and stops in 28 su. It
  can't turn in place: it turns on a circle of one grid unit, and drives a loop forward to reach a point behind it.
  Its machine gun is on a turret that turns all the way round (12° a tick), so it fires while it drives, at ground
  and air (3 damage, 4 grid units, a shot every 4 ticks, never misses). Its armour is directional: hits from the
  front do less (× 0.75), from behind more (× 1.25). It can't drive onto Rugged tiles. Recruits repair it (D, or a
  right-click on it when it's damaged): 60 to 120 HP takes 80 frames with one Recruit and 40 with two, 19 crystals
  either way.
- **W: Reverse.** Its panel is the combat units' with W Reverse: W, then a click on the ground, and it backs up
  there in a straight line, slowly (3 su a tick). It is the only way it backs up.
- **Jams, a thing to watch.** In a crowd, a Seeker can jam for good where its turn is cut and nothing makes way. It
  can't turn in place, it backs up only on W, and enemies and idle vehicles never step aside (0353). In V2's soak,
  295 of 452 moves failed that way. The doorway can jam the same way, but only with the rally point cleared: then
  the first Seeker stops just outside side C, and the next one waits in the doorway behind it, the queue paused,
  until you move the first (an idle vehicle doesn't make way). With the default rally point, three Seekers fit
  between it and the door; a fourth left alone stops in the doorway behind them, and a fifth waits inside, until you
  move them or the rally point.

Try: Seekers driving past Militants and Guards, firing on the move; Seekers against Sparrows (their gun hits air) and
against Rocketmen (their missiles and locks hit vehicles too); attacks from behind against attacks from the front; a
crowd of Seekers in a narrow pass, and W to back one out of a jam; a new Chop Shop's rally point, turned every way
and placed against the map's edge; three Seekers queued without touching the rally point, then five; five queued
with the rally point cleared; the Chop Shop destroyed with Seekers queued (the Recruits come out, the orders
refunded, a finished Seeker comes out as a Seeker).

## Crushing (slice M5-V4, design changes 0418 and 0419)

- **Who crushes.** Tracked vehicles (the Hand of Heaven, built at the Chop Shop: below) and Mechs crush the enemy's
  Light Infantry (Recruits, Militants, Peacekeepers, …): driving forward, backing up on W, or turning on the spot,
  the tank kills at once every enemy infantryman its hull runs over or swings into, and drives on as if they
  weren't there. A crush shows as any death.
- **Who doesn't get crushed.** Your own infantry (and an ally's, once there are allies): they stop the tank, and it
  goes round them; turning on the spot, it stops turning when its hull touches one. Heavy Infantry (Guards) and
  vehicles are never crushed. A tank that stands and doesn't turn is a wall to everyone: enemy infantry walks round
  it. A tank braking to a stop crushes nothing.
- **The Subterranean Transport** will crush above ground only, once it has its above-ground mode (slice M6-ST1).
  Underground it never crushes.

Try: drive a Hand of Heaven through a line of enemy Peacekeepers (they all die, it doesn't swerve); park one with an
enemy infantryman against its side and order it to face him; the same with your own Recruit on Hold (the tank stops
turning, nobody dies); back one up on W through enemy infantry; send it through a mixed crowd of your own and enemy
infantry, and through enemy Guards.

## The Hand of Heaven (slice M5-N10, design changes 0361, 0363 and 0444)

- **Getting one.** At the Chop Shop (above), pick a kind of Recruit, then W builds a Hand of Heaven (150 crystals,
  30 s) of **three** Recruits of that kind, the three inside the longest. W is greyed while fewer than three of the
  kind are free ("No free unit of that kind") or while you lack the crystals; its hint reads "W: Build Hand of Heaven
  (150 Space Crystals)". X cancels it with a full refund, its three Recruits back in the pool. It comes out of side C
  like a Seeker, facing out, at full HP, and drives to the rally point. Its Unit Control is 3. The computer players
  don't build one.
- **Its look.** A 64 × 40 hull labelled "HOH", a dark tread down each long side, and its launcher: the turret barrel,
  which turns only 10° either way on the hull. All placeholders.
- **Driving.** Tracked: up to 5 su a tick, speeding up by 0.5 a tick. Stopped, it spins on the spot (about 19° a
  tick); at full speed it can't turn. W: Reverse backs it up at 2.5 su a tick. 200 HP, armour 2, sight 7 grid units.
- **Aiming.** Attack something in range but outside its launcher's 20° and it stops and spins its hull on the spot
  until the target is inside, then aims for 3 s: a quarter turn takes 5 ticks, a half turn 9. Out of range, it drives
  into range first. Left alone, it does the same for an enemy its auto-targeting picks. On Hold it never turns: its
  launcher follows a target to the edge of its 20° and lets it go. Turning on the spot it crushes the enemy infantry
  its hull swings into (Crushing, above); your own idle infantry in the way steps aside. Against a wall it creeps
  off it as it turns, a few su, and aims a little later.
- **The missile.** Range 14 grid units, but nothing inside 4: closer enemies can't be targeted, and an Attack on one
  makes it back off first. It sees only 7, so beyond that it needs a spotter (a Recruit nearby, say). The missile is
  slow (8 su a tick: 6 s at 12 tiles, about 8.2 s at full range against a 4 × 4 building) and doesn't home: whatever
  walks off the spot dodges it. It lands as a 96 su blast (the ring) that hits everything in the circle, your own
  units too: 600 damage over the circle, so a 3 × 3 building inside it takes about 593 (a Chop Shop falls to one), a
  lone infantryman about 12. It can't be shot down. D: Attack Ground, then a click, fires at a spot again and again
  until another order.
- **Shoot and scoot.** Once the missile is away (2 ticks of firing, then 4 of cooldown), a Move drives it off at once
  while its 6 s reload runs; the missile lands anyway. The enemy sees it when its missile lands, not when it fires.
- **Placeholders.** The look above, the missile drawn as a plain ball on the cosmetic lob every projectile but a
  grenade or the Rocketman's missile has, with its shadow under it, and the blast ring of every blast.
- **A thing to watch.** Alone, attacking an enemy inside its 4 grid units, it backs off to the middle of its band, 9
  grid units, beyond its own sight of 7: it loses sight of the enemy on the way and the Attack ends there, unfired,
  about 6.8 grid units off. With a spotter it backs off and fires.

Try: a Hand of Heaven with a Recruit spotting for it, shelling a building from 12 to 14 tiles, then driving off as
each missile leaves; one ordered at a building behind it (it spins on the spot); missiles at a walking Militant (it
dodges) and at an enemy next to your own units (they are hit too); Attack Ground on a choke point; an enemy walking
up inside 4 tiles; on Hold, an enemy crossing in front of it; and the enemy's view: a missile out of the dark, and
the Hand of Heaven revealed as it lands.
