# Q0202 — An aircraft landed in the field: do ground units pass under it?

- **Date**: 2026-11-28T07:48:21-05:00
- **Blocking**: no — proceeding on (a), as built in R1b: ground units pass under it
- **Docs**: `systems/mechanics/aircraft_landing.md > Landed aircraft (every archetype)` and `> Field landing`; `systems/mechanics/carrying.md` (the Landing carrier)

## Question
An aircraft lands in the field: a Landing carrier, such as the Supply Chopper, comes down on open ground to load or unload. While it is landed, does it block ground units as a ground unit of its size would? Or do ground units pass under it, as under a flying aircraft?

## Context
R1b built Field landing. The aircraft lands at a free point of open ground, or the nearest free one.

The design lists what changes when an aircraft lands:
- it becomes a ground target;
- it holds fire;
- it leaves the air separation.

It says nothing about ground movement. As built, the landed aircraft stays on the air layer: ground units walk through its silhouette, and it never pushes them. Aircraft that land on a structure (the Airfield's slots, the Supply Tower's spot) never meet ground units, so the question first arises with Field landing. R2, the carrier phase, is next to build on it.

## Options
- **(a)** As built: ground units pass under it. A landed Chopper never blocks a path, and units boarding it walk to its centre.
- **(b)** While landed, from the end of its landing to the start of its take-off, it blocks ground units as a ground unit of its silhouette would. Ground units path around it, and those boarding walk to its edge. The landing rule already keeps its spot clear, so it never lands on a unit.
